Exact laws. Unit-cost coordinate queries. Ideal arithmetic.

What does the demonstration represent?

For n coordinates θᵢ in [0,1], the two-output family is

Pθ(1) = η + (1 − 2η) max((k − 1)/n, 1 − ∑θᵢ/n).

Here η = 0.1 and θ = 0. The unrestricted target certificate returns without a query with probability 0.2; otherwise it queries a uniformly chosen coordinate. The maximal-common certificate returns with probability 0.2 + 0.8(k − 1)/n; otherwise it queries a uniformly chosen k-subset.

The shared draw chooses the branch and output. Independent subset randomness is represented by the displayed subset. The diagram omits the residual completion that makes the policies exact away from this target. It is not a universally exact sampler implementation. Randomize uses a finite display grid; the theorem uses continuous randomness.

What is free, and what is counted?

Each previously unknown coordinate costs one query to reveal exactly. Computation, randomness and the required envelope metadata are free in this oracle model. Policies must produce the specified finite output law at every feasible input.

The pointwise optimum can choose a different globally exact policy for each target. The construction is an existence result and does not claim efficient computation of arbitrary envelopes. Query counts do not imply latency, hardware or floating-point guarantees.

What do the bounds actually say?

The general upper bound is (n + 1)²/(4n). Maximizing the displayed lower family gives floor((n + 1)²/4)/n. They coincide for odd n; their difference is 1/(4n) for even n. A separate sharper argument gives ρ₂ = 1.

The categorical factor-two result assumes independent intervals with finite positive endpoints and equal query costs. It is a separate structured class. It does not say the general price is two.

Does this depend on target-specific policies?

A separate construction compares single policies exact on the entire domain. Under a uniform product prior, imposing maximal no-query mass can have a linear average-query penalty, although an exact baseline reads at most two coordinates per execution and one in expectation.
